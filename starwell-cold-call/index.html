Every ring answered

StarWell sets up a virtual receptionist for the calls you miss. It books the job or transfers emergencies to your on-call tech, then texts you a summary.

Say "phone answering" or "virtual receptionist." Never lead with "AI."

Why it matters

  • 27% of home-services calls went unanswered (Invoca, 2024).
  • Under 3% of callers sent to voicemail leave a message (Invoca).
  • 46% of home-services phone leads book on the call (Invoca, 2025).

The call flow

  1. Greeting + Connecticut's recording notice (CGS §52-570d).
  2. Emergencies first: gas smell or CO alarm → leave the home, call 911 or the gas utility → live transfer to the on-call tech.
  3. Booking details, one question at a time.
  4. Summary texted to the owner.

Rollout: a test number first. Week one: learn, build, practice calls with the owner, soft launch, tune. His line forwards only with his yes.

Missed-call money: use his numbers only. Missed calls × real jobs × his booking rate × his ticket. Never quote our own figure.

Psychology · the caller

Your confidence kit

  1. They like you more than you think. After first conversations, people underestimate how much the other person liked them (Boothby et al., 2018).
  2. Call nerves "excitement." Saying "I'm excited" reliably raises how excited people feel (Brooks, 2014); the performance boost is less certain.
  3. Your voice is the demo. Slow down, smile, pause after the reason.
  4. You hold a gift. You're not asking for a favor.
  5. "Not now" is timing. Thank him, move on, keep your energy.
  6. Rhythm beats mood. Call daily, log within the hour (Jeb Blount).

Call structure

Five steps, two minutes

The longest step is the one where he talks.
Step 1 · 10 seconds

Say why you're calling

Hi Craig, this is with StarWell, here in Connecticut. This is a cold call, so I'll be quick. I was on your website this morning and noticed one thing I think could help.

Practitioner Gong: permission-style openers ~11% success vs ~2% for "Did I catch you at a bad time?"

Mentor
John Barrows: say the reason for your call. Never "just checking in."

Step 3 · 30–60 seconds · strongest evidence

Ask about last week

When that first cold night hit last week, how did the phones go after hours?

Direct Adaptive selling, shaping the talk around the buyer, improved real sales performance across 155 samples and 31,000+ salespeople (Franke & Park, 2006).

  1. Follow up once: "You said Sarah handles callbacks. What about her day off?"
  2. Ask his advice: "Thirty years in Connecticut HVAC: what do homeowners get wrong?"
  3. Let him do the math: "What's a typical no-heat repair worth to you?"

"Last week" gets facts. "Would you ever…" gets polite guesses.

Mentors
Josh Braun: one neutral question beats any pitch. Neil Rackham: people commit when they name the cost.

Lead with what fits

StarWell built a demo line that answers like your office, books the call and texts you. It's a separate test number. Call it tonight.

Transferred Naming the motive answers "what's the catch" before he asks (Friestad & Wright, 1994). One honest limit makes the rest believable (Eisend, 2006).

One gift in focus. The rest in one line. Then stop talking.
Step 5 · 15 seconds

Email, then a date

What's the best email? I'll send it within the hour. Could I walk you through it Tuesday at 7:30, before the trucks go out? Fifteen minutes. If it's not useful, say so and I won't follow up.

Transferred Specific when-and-where plans strongly raise follow-through (94 tests, Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).

Our way

Talk like a good steward

Every answer: Thank → Truth (including what we don't know) → Tie it to his shop → Turn it back with a question.
  • Follow-up questions make people like you more (Huang et al., 2017).
  • Repeat his words back. Servers who repeated orders verbatim got bigger tips (van Baaren et al., 2003).
  • Summarize, then offer: "So nights and Saturdays slip. That's what the demo is for."
  • Analogies from his trade. Make him the hero; StarWell is the guide.
  • Pause after the reason and after why-free.
  • "I'm not sure, I'll text you by tonight." Honest uncertainty, said confidently, keeps trust (Gaertig & Simmons, 2018).
  • Begin in a friendly way.
  • Let him do most of the talking.
  • Avoid arguments; never say "you're wrong."
  • If we're wrong, admit it quickly.
  • See it from his point of view.
  • Let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay Matthew 5:37
  • Swift to hear, slow to speak James 1:19
  • A soft answer turneth away wrath Proverbs 15:1
  • Go with him twain Matthew 5:41 · the free package is our second mile
  • Faithful in that which is least Luke 16:10 · keep small promises

King James Version.

  • Value equation: show the result, make it believable, cut his time and effort.
  • Give away the secrets, sell the implementation.
  • Make the offer so good there's no need to push.

Good questions. Straight answers.

Great question. The package StarWell built is free, and it stays yours. If you ever want us to keep it running, most shops start around $300 to $400 a month, in writing, no surprises. We'd rather see it work for you first.
  • Depends on: locations and trucks, call volume, how much he wants us to run.
