Stop 1 · Fear is normal

Their fear is smart.

When an owner says "I don't trust AI," they aren't being difficult. Losing something hurts more than gaining something feels good. Psychologists Kahneman and Tversky showed this in 1979, and called it loss aversion.

For an owner, the loss is real: a leaked customer list, a wrong price, a bad review. One mistake can undo years of word of mouth.

So treat fear as a smart instinct to respect, not an objection to beat.

We've been here before

Every big tool scared people first.

The pattern

Fear, then locks, then normal.

Each time, fear eased because of better locks, not by avoiding the tool. With AI, building the locks is our job from day one.

Never say this

Lines that break trust.

"That's just hype, don't worry.""That's a fair worry. Here's exactly how we handle it."Dismissing fear tells them they weren't heard.
"It's 100% secure.""No system is perfect, so we keep its access small and a person checks the big stuff."One broken promise costs more than ten honest ones earn.
"If you wait, you'll fall behind.""There's no rush. We'll start small when you're ready."Scaring someone into a yes is the opposite of serving them.

Practice what we preach

  • Never paste customer data into a free chatbot

HEAR it

  1. Hear it all, without interrupting
  2. Empathize: name the feeling
  3. Answer with the lock
  4. Return control to them

Never

  • Dismiss, overpromise, rush, or hide behind jargon

Follow through

  • Put what it can and can't do in writing
  • Start with one small thing, like after-hours calls
  • Review the call log together
  • If it isn't helping, tell them
