Stop 3 · Listen and capture

Listen for six signals.

Owners rarely say "this is my biggest problem." They signal it. Tap each to hear it.

Spot the signals

Mike (an example owner) says this. Find the signals before you tap.

Dig first: after-hours calls (said twice).

Ask: how many of the 15 quotes become jobs?

Play it back

Get them to say "That's right."

Before you leave, sum up what you heard in their words. In a 2010 study, people felt more understood when a listener paraphrased them than when the listener gave advice.

"So what I'm hearing is . It happens , and it's costing you . Did I get that right?"

Negotiator Chris Voss: "That's right" means they feel understood. "You're right" often means "please stop." If you hear it, try again in their words.

Stop 4 · What matters most

Pebble or cracked heat exchanger?

A pebble in a condenser rattles loudly but is harmless. A cracked heat exchanger is quiet but can leak carbon monoxide into the home, so a tech shuts the furnace down. Owners' problems work the same way.

Loudest isn't costliest. Ask: "What do you just put up with?"

Stop 5 · AI and promises

What AI should touch.

Why: words or data on a screen, repeated daily, and a mistake is cheap to catch.

  • Answer after-hours calls and text back
  • Follow up on sent quotes
  • Review requests and tune-up reminders
  • Turn tech notes into clean invoices

Why: AI drafts, a person approves, because a mistake costs money or trust.

  • Dispatch suggestions and ETA texts
  • Quote drafts from past jobs
  • Payment reminders (a person handles disputes)
  • Rebate paperwork drafts

Why: safety, judgment or emotions. A mistake could hurt someone.

  • Gas smell or CO calls: straight to a person or 911
  • Diagnosing equipment remotely
  • Final pricing decisions
  • Upset customers in a dispute

How AI connects

It can only use what it can reach.

Think of it like wiring: some software has an outlet ready, some needs an adapter, some has no power at all. Ask what software they use before promising anything.

Never overpromise

Quote after the diagnosis.

Never say "100% accurate." Say: "It will make mistakes. Here's how we'll catch them."

Mindset

Set up

  • Ask before the note taker records

The 8 questions

  1. How did you get into HVAC?
  2. Walk me through yesterday.
  3. Where do things slow down or slip?
  4. When did that last happen? Then what?
  5. What do you just put up with?
  6. What have you tried before?
  7. If fixed, what changes for you?
  8. What would make you nervous?

Follow up

  • Tell me more · What happened next? · What causes that? · Mirror · Pause 3 sec

Before you leave

  • Play it back in their words
  • Ask: "Did I get that right?"
  • Know / Think / Wouldn't, never overpromise

After

  • Let the AI turn the transcript into notes
  • Recap to the owner within 24 hours, in their words
