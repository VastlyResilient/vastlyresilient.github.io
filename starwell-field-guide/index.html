Listen for six signals.

Owners rarely say "this is my biggest problem." They signal it.

Play it back

Get them to say "That's right."

Before you leave, sum up what you heard in their words. In a 2010 study, people felt more understood when a listener paraphrased them than when the listener gave advice.

"So what I'm hearing is . It happens , and it's costing you . Did I get that right?"

Negotiator Chris Voss: "That's right" means they feel understood. "You're right" often means "please stop." If you hear it, try again in their words.

Stop 4 · What matters most

Pebble or cracked heat exchanger?

A pebble in a condenser rattles loudly but is harmless. A cracked heat exchanger is quiet but can leak carbon monoxide into the home, so a tech shuts the furnace down. Owners' problems work the same way.

PebbleLoud. Harmless.In a business: "Our website font looks dated." Annoying, costs nothing real. Note it, move on.
Must-fixQuiet. Dangerous.In a business: calls go to voicemail after 5pm and customers book someone else. Quiet, but it costs jobs every week.

Loudest isn't costliest. Ask: "What do you just put up with?"

Owners act on dollars.

Stop 5 · AI and promises · How AI connects

It can only use what it can reach.

Like wiring: some software has an outlet, some needs an adapter, some has no power. Ask what they use before promising anything.

Never overpromise

Quote after the diagnosis.

Trust is lost faster by one broken promise than by ten honest "let me check" answers. Put every idea into one of three buckets, and say which bucket it's in.

We know we can

"Calls after 5pm get answered, and you get a text summary each morning. We've set this up before."

We think we can

"Booking straight into your schedule should work with your software. We'll confirm it on a test before you rely on it."

We wouldn't

"A gas-smell call should always reach a person. I wouldn't hand that to AI."

What to say on a first visit

Promise the next step, not the result.

The only promise on visit one

"Let me go through what you told me. I'll come back with what's possible, what isn't, and what I'd try first."

"AI will handle everything.""AI can take the first reply. Your team handles the rest."
"It's 100% accurate.""We'll review the results with you each week and adjust as we go."
"This will double your revenue.""Let's measure missed calls now, so we can both see if it helps."

Field card

Mindset

  • Look to their interests, not just yours (Phil. 2:4)
  • Be the tech: diagnose before you prescribe
  • Goal: they say "That's right"

Set up

  • Ask before the note taker records
  • Phone face-down, out of view
  • Plan ~40 min: Open, Map, Dig, Future

The 8 questions

  1. How did you get into HVAC?
  2. Walk me through yesterday.
  3. Where do things slow down or slip?
  4. When did that last happen? Then what?
  5. What do you just put up with?
  6. What have you tried before?
  7. If fixed, what changes for you?
  8. What would make you nervous?

Follow up

  • Tell me more · What happened next? · What causes that? · Mirror · Pause 3 sec

Before you leave

  • Play it back in their words
  • Ask: "Did I get that right?"
  • Know / Think / Wouldn't, never overpromise

After

  • Let the AI turn the transcript into notes
  • Recap to the owner within 24 hours, in their words
