Stop 1 · Mindset

Be the tech, not the salesperson.

On the first visit you have one job: understand the business so well that the owner says, "That's right."

Think of a service call

The tech checks the system before quoting. The salesperson shows up with a unit already picked out. Be the tech.

Why it works on people

Respect opens people up.

Criticize their setup and they defend it. Ask about it and they explain it.

Dale Carnegie, 1936
Talking feels good.

Sharing about ourselves lights up the brain's reward system. Let them talk.

Tamir & Mitchell, Harvard, 2012
Follow-ups build trust.

People who ask more follow-ups are liked more. It proves you heard them.

Huang et al., Harvard, 2017

Owners describe fixes, not problems

"We need a chatbot" is their guess at a fix. Your job is to find the problem underneath it.

OwnerWe need a chatbot on the website.

YouWhat would that fix for you?

OwnerHonestly? We miss calls at night.

Now you're working on the real problem, and a chatbot might not even be the best fix.

Rule of thumb

You talk less than half the time. The last 7 minutes are for playing back what you heard and booking the next step.

Three rules to carry in

They're the expert on their shop. You're the expert on tools.

Your knowledge only matters once it's pointed at their real problem.

Curious beats clever.

You don't need to sound smart. You need to ask the next good question.

Understand first. Sell later, if at all.

If AI isn't the right fix, say so. That honesty is why they call you back.

Stop 2 · Ask

Climb the ladder.

Ask in order: what happens, what goes wrong, what it costs, what they want. Skip a rung and you're guessing.

After any answer, follow up

"Tell me more about that.""What happened next?""How often does that happen?""Can you give me a recent example?"Repeat their last three words

Then stay quiet for three seconds

Silence feels awkward to you. To the owner it feels like room to keep talking. The most useful thing they say often comes right after a pause.

Fix the weak question

Stop 3 · Listen

Listen for six signals

The pain is rarely in the first answer. It shows up in these.

RepeatsSaid three times = their real priority
Workarounds"We just…" = hidden manual work
Energy shiftsA sigh, laugh or louder voice = it hurts
NumbersCalls, hours, dollars: write them down
One person holds it"Only Linda knows how" = a bottleneck
"Always done it this way"Habit or pride? Ask gently.

Play it back

"So what I'm hearing is the problem, in their words. It happens how often, and it costs you time, money or stress. Did I get that right?"

Aim for "That's right."

Negotiator Chris Voss: "That's right" means they feel understood. "You're right" often means they want you to stop talking.

Label and mirror

Label the feeling

It sounds like evenings are where this really hurts.

If you're wrong, they'll correct you and tell you more.

YouGoes to voicemail?

Mirror the last words

Repeat their last few words as a question, then wait. They'll explain without you steering.

Stop 4 · Sort

Pebble or cracked heat exchanger?

A squeaky blower is annoying. A cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide. Sort before you solve.

Pebble

Annoying, but costs no real money or customers.

Must-fix

Loses money, customers or safety every week.

Two traps

Loudest isn't largest.

The thing they complain about with the most emotion can still be a pebble. Check it with numbers.

The silent must-fix.

Owners don't mention what they've accepted as "part of the job." They don't know it can be fixed. Ask:

What do you just put up with?

If you had one extra office person for free, what would you hand them first?

The pain test

Must-fix

Try:

Put a number on it

Owners act on dollars and hours. Run the math with them, out loud, using their numbers.

Per year$54,600
200 hours a year$6,000

Example numbers. Always use the owner's own.

Find the bottleneck

A duct moves only as much air as its tightest point. Tap a stage.

Symptom or cause?

A frozen coil is a symptom. Low airflow is the cause. Keep asking "What causes that?" until you reach a process, not a person.

We miss calls.
Techs are on jobs.
There's one office person.
No backup in peak season.
Root cause: nobody is assigned to the phones after 5pm.

Toyota made this habit famous as "ask why five times."

Stop 5 · Can AI fix it?

Check the panel before you install.

An electrician checks the panel before adding a circuit. Check the fit before you suggest any AI.

AI is like a new office hire

Fast and tireless. Great with words and data. Needs rules and someone checking its work. Can't climb a ladder, and sometimes sounds confident when it's wrong.

Three checks before you suggest

Is it work done with words or data, on a screen or a phone?

Answering, scheduling, reminding: yes. Installing or diagnosing at the unit: no.

Does it repeat with clear rules, and can a mistake be caught?

A wrong reminder text is cheap. A wrong price on a $14,000 install is not.

Can we reach the information?

Where does the data live, and can we read and write it? That's the plug-in ladder.

What fits in an HVAC shop

  • Answer after-hours calls and text back
  • Follow up on sent quotes
  • Ask for reviews after a job
  • Maintenance and tune-up reminders
  • Turn tech notes into clean invoices
  • Call summaries for the office
  • Dispatch suggestions and ETA texts
  • Draft quotes from past jobs; owner approves
  • Payment reminders; a person handles disputes
  • Rebate paperwork drafts (Energize CT); a person submits
  • Screening job applicants
  • Gas smell or CO calls: straight to a person or 911
  • Diagnosing equipment remotely
  • Final pricing decisions
  • Work that needs a license
  • Upset customers in a dispute

The plug-in ladder

How AI reaches their software, easiest first. Tap a rung for what to check.

Start at the lowest rung

Pick the simplest connection that solves the problem. Upgrade once it's proven.

Week 1
Forward missed calls to an AI agent

Plain call forwarding. It answers, takes details, and texts the owner a summary. No integration needed.

Week 3
Measure against the baseline

Missed calls before vs. after. The owner sees the number.

Later
Book straight into the schedule

Needs the API rung. Only once the owner's plan and admin allow it.

Common HVAC software

As of October 2026. None has an official MCP connector. Always confirm inside the owner's account.

Ask about their systems

What software runs the business, and which plan are you on?

Who's the admin on that account?

Can you pull a report of last month's calls or jobs?

Is anything still on paper or a whiteboard?

MCP, in plain words

A 2024 open standard from Anthropic that lets AI plug into software one common way, like USB-C. No official one? Use the API.

Stop 6 · Promise

Quote after the diagnosis.

Sort every idea into three buckets, and say them out loud.

We know we can

This works today. We've seen it work.

Answering after-hours calls with a text summary.
We think we can

This should work. Here's how we'll test it.

Booking into your schedule, once we confirm your plan.
We wouldn't

I'd keep this human, and here's why.

Gas smell calls, final pricing, disputes.

Prove it small first

Measure today

Missed calls over the last two weeks.

Pick one problem

The must-fix with the clearest number.

Run it two weeks

A person checks the AI's work daily at first.

Compare

Same number, before and after.

Decide together

Keep, adjust or stop. The owner decides.

Say this instead

"AI will handle everything.""AI handles the first reply. Your team handles the rest."
"It's 100% accurate.""It will make mistakes. Here's how we'll catch them."
"Set it and forget it.""We'll check it weekly for the first month."
"You won't need your office manager.""She gets her Friday afternoons back."
"We can connect to anything.""Let me check what your software allows first."
